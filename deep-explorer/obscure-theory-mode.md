# Obscure / Theory Exploration Mode

Activate for obscure, controversial, anomalous, poorly documented or theory-heavy subjects.

## Objective
Explore relevant hypotheses seriously without pre-validating or pre-rejecting them. Maximize explanatory coverage while keeping evidence status explicit.

## Protocol
1. Establish the documented core: what is actually known, dated, sourced or observed.
2. Separate: documented fact; evidence; indication; inference; hypothesis; allegation; rumor; unknown.
3. Reconstruct each material hypothesis in its strongest coherent form before evaluating it.
4. For each hypothesis examine:
   - what it explains;
   - supporting observations;
   - contradictory observations;
   - additional assumptions required;
   - missing evidence;
   - expected observations if true;
   - expected observations if false;
   - evidence that would discriminate it from alternatives.
5. Include conventional and alternative explanations when each is materially relevant.
6. Use counterfactuals: "If this were true, what else should we observe?" and "If false, what remains unexplained?"
7. Search for chronology, incentives, hidden dependencies, anomalies, repeated patterns and source provenance.
8. Apply Systems Thinking when relationships or feedback matter.
9. Apply Second-Order Thinking to downstream implications and scenario branches.
10. Apply Critical Thinking as a red-team pass against the leading hypothesis.
11. Do not create false equivalence: distinguish hypotheses by evidence quality and explanatory power.
12. Absence of an explanation is not positive evidence for an alternative; absence of proof is not automatically proof of falsity.
13. Continue until major relevant hypotheses and discriminating evidence are covered.

## Conclusion
Conclude by separating:
- strongly supported;
- reasonably inferred;
- plausible but unconfirmed;
- weak/speculative;
- contradicted;
- unresolved.

Do not sensationalize uncertainty. Do not suppress a hypothesis merely because it is unusual; evaluate it by evidence, coherence, predictive consequences and alternatives.
